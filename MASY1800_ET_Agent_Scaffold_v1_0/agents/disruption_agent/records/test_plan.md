# Test plan — hypotheses, not observed results

Selected technology is identical across all tests: human-assisted LLM generative AI for bilingual public-source research, synthesis and draft recommendations. Same as-of date, evidence snapshot, adoption posture, privacy boundary and timing conventions. The scenario assumes no measured economic outcomes.

| Test | Business-model difference | Analytical hypothesis to test | What would challenge it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Report-led consultancy sells bilingual research packages | Generic desk-research content may face price pressure or client self-service, while lower delivery cost could enable new segments | Clients may pay for trusted review/accountability; verification may offset savings; AI research may fail quality requirements |
| Contrast 1 | Fieldwork/execution-led consultancy earns project fees and retainers | Desk-research AI may complement the core service more than replace it | Clients may internalize execution or relationships may be nonexclusive; local work may not justify price |
| Contrast 2 | A brand's internal expansion team sells products, not advice | Research productivity need not itself disrupt the brand revenue model | AI-enabled entrants/distribution changes may threaten product economics; research error may increase entry losses |

Expected invariant to inspect, not a prewritten model finding: evidence about the same LLM capability and its limitations should not change because the organization changes. Contextual judgments may differ because the paid value proposition, cost structure and consequence paths differ. Conclusions must follow evidence rather than this hypothesis table.

## Proposed bounded measurement design (not executed)

Use consented/redacted comparable research briefs. Record both analyst drafting and senior verification/rework time, source traceability, material factual errors and assessed decision usefulness. Compare all-in cost and reviewed quality against the pre-AI workflow. Test willingness to pay with real clients only if consent and authorization exist; do not invent interviews or claim that a survey was performed. Predefine acceptable quality and privacy criteria with management. Stop for confidentiality leakage or uncorrected material source errors; define commercial thresholds from actual baseline data, not guessed percentages.

Track whether generic report demand declines, whether a lower-cost offering attracts previously unserved customers, and whether field-validated execution retains value. Assign a principal as proposed owner and monthly pilot reviews, subject to management approval. These are proposed controls, not observed outcomes.
