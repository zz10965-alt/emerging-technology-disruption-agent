## Three levels of analysis

- **ET generally:** Human-assisted LLM synthesis/drafting is plausible, with confabulation, privacy and overreliance risks. Task improvement alone is not Christensen-style disruption; a low-end/new-market foothold and movement into incumbent demand would need evidence.
- **For this application:** Public competitor scans and bilingual briefs may become easier to produce, but authentic consumer validation and source verification remain distinct. Net cost after review/rework is unknown.
- **For this organization:** Selling generic report content may expose the paid core to self-service substitution. Trusted judgment, verification or local insight could sustain fees, but neither their pricing power nor actual client switching is measured. The opportunity to serve smaller underserved brands is conditional on demand and viable economics.

## Incumbent dilemma and reassessment

The firm may need to stop protecting hours spent assembling generic reports and invest in auditable synthesis and genuinely validated insight. Moving too early risks uncompensated checking or premature fee cannibalization; waiting risks losing clients to acceptable substitutes. Neither outcome is measured. Human source checks and approved public data remain necessary, with controls themselves tested rather than presumed effective.

The principal should review client interviews and lost bids attributed to self-service, with a defined denominator, sample minimum and calibrated threshold. Source freshness, material errors, rework and confidentiality incidents should be reviewed at pilot checkpoints. A material breach pauses use pending human review. These are proposed controls, not observed triggers.
